Dusk 🌆 — the most exhausting hour of the day. Plenty to tend to, everyone needing a response, everything needing care.

I'm used to being the one who holds it all together — strong-willed, independent, digesting whatever comes on my own. I rarely trouble others, and I don't easily show weakness. Self-respect and independence are the bottom lines I hold most dear.

The phone screen lit up — my child's call. As I answered, I instinctively tucked away my fatigue, keeping my voice as steady as I could, chatting about everyday things like always. But as we talked, the weariness piled up inside me, and the longing for a certain unspoken understanding, seeped out all the same.

As I see it, some emotional bonds should never need to be spelled out — the moment they turn into explicit instructions, that natural attunement shatters.

But my child is used to a different way of communicating — one that values logic, facts, and solving problems.

There's nothing wrong with that; it's even a sign of great maturity. But in that moment, talking on completely different wavelengths left my feelings suddenly with nowhere to land. What did it feel like? Like reaching out with something tender, only to hit a wall of reason.

In that moment, there was only one thought: retreat!

I didn't want to say another word, didn't want to try to explain. I quickly gathered myself and ended the call in a hurry.

The instant the phone went silent, the whole house sank into a near-vacuum of stillness.

I sat there feeling hollowed out in an instant. Utter exhaustion, appetite gone without a trace. A tidal wave of inner friction swept over me — I can satisfy myself, I need no material extras, so why insist on chasing that elusive, intangible "feeling"?

In that moment, two words surfaced in my heart: "beggar."

Someone who prides herself on being independent and graceful, yet at the door of emotion, like a beggar holding out her hand for crumbs. That feeling of reaching outward and coming back empty-handed left my dignity a little battered.

I sat with it alone for a long time...

The old philosophical definition of love: To love is to will the good of the other — love is, in essence, the firm resolve of the will to seek the good of the other.

A knot that had been pulled tight inside me quietly loosened, just a corner...

I always thought I was a generous gardener, giving tirelessly — yet deep down, I was still waiting for that garden to give off the fragrance I expected. When the response fell short, I felt disappointed.

But the truth is, my child has their own world and their own way of expressing things.

Their love is reason, is order — taking good care of themselves, never troubling their elders. That is already, in their own way, a deeply sincere love.

There's no lack of delicate sensitivity here — just different habits of expression, neither higher nor lower than the other.

Going to a place that sells no flowers to buy flowers — coming back empty-handed is only reasonable.

I don't need to blame my own high sensitivity, for sensitivity and depth let me see more of life's exquisite beauty; nor should I ask my child to change. Honoring each other's differences — that is the loveliest thing.

And I need not feel small or blame myself for a moment's emotional shortfall.

Each of us needs to build a sanctuary for our own soul.

This "sanctuary" is not some relative who must fully understand you, nor some flawless emotional response. This "sanctuary" is where we finally decide to withdraw the outstretched, begging hand, and gently take back into our own arms that wounded, wronged, yet fiercely proud "me."

Here, I can admit I'm tired, I can hold my disappointment, and I can lay down every burden I've been carrying by sheer force.

When I no longer reach outward for that particular "feeling," when I allow everyone to exist in their own way, I no longer need to make things hard for myself — and at last, a long-missing calm returns within.

Night deepened; moonlight lay like water outside the window.

Tomorrow the sun will rise as always, and I will still be my fiery, cool-to-the-core self. Only this time, I am the gentle sanctuary of my own soul — no longer anyone's "beggar."
