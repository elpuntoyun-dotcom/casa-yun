CASA YUN NOTES

Running Toward the Goal, Once Again

On "family of origin," overcompensating out of fear, and the quiet grace of pressing on toward the goal.

October 14, 2026

Over the past few days, a specific scripture has crossed my path repeatedly: "Running toward the goal." Perhaps it is no coincidence, but a gentle reminder intended for this moment.

Recently, I've noticed more people around me talking about their "family of origin."

Someone brought up that famous verse: "Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal." He rarely spoke of his parents, only sharing how he had moved on, experienced small miracles, and felt deeply blessed by God.

Hearing those words, I simply smiled inside—a quiet, knowing smile.

Those who understand need no explanation. Only those who have truly walked through the wilderness can sense the subtle, unresolved waves beneath words that sound overly passionate or picture-perfect.

1. "Family of Origin": An Abstract Refuge

When a person's childhood is filled with warmth, speaking of parents usually comes with concrete, grounded memories of daily life. But when someone frequently defaults to the abstract concept of "family of origin," it often encapsulates memories too bitter, painful, or traumatic to face directly.

As time goes on, everyone seeks healing in their own way. Some find new life through a loving partner, while others find solace in faith. These are genuine blessings. Yet sometimes, beneath the loud declarations of being "the happiest person alive," you can feel a subtle sense of trying too hard.

2. Overcompensating Out of Fear

The more fervently someone tries to prove a point, the more it reveals a deep-seated lack from the past.

This isn't hypocrisy; it's a natural mechanism of self-protection. Having experienced helplessness that cut to the bone, when a tiny beam of light finally appears—a welcoming home, a quiet sanctuary—they cling to it like a swimmer holding onto a lifeline, declaring to the world: "I am safe. I am doing wonderful."

Behind that exaggerated sense of joy often lies a quiet anxiety: the lingering fear of losing it all.

3. Gently, Pressing On

Understanding this isn't about tearing down anyone's armor. It is about recognizing the fragile light and subtle heartbreak inherent in human nature, allowing room for empathy and gentleness.

Those who loudly proclaim they have "forgotten what is behind" may never truly have forgotten. Yet, in their own way, they are living bravely, offering a long-overdue sense of comfort to the helpless child they once were.

"Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal."

As this scripture echoes once more, I choose to gather my thoughts and move forward. With a quiet grace that comes from truly seeing life for what it is, I press on toward the goal—gently and steadily.
